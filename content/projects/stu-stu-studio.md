## Before

This was a 500 sq ft detached two-car garage with a prominent overhang at the entrance.

## After

We created a 700 sq ft studio with exposed rafters and an intentional floor plan that makes the most of the available space.

## The Story

This renovation turned a dusty old garage into something truly special. We split the existing space under the overhang into two: framing in the left half to create a sunroom wrapped in windows, and opening the right half for a front porch. Several 4x4 posts reinforce the structure and define the space, while curtains wrap the porch to create privacy when desired.

We matched the existing Hardie board siding to keep the exterior consistent. When you enter the front door, the whole space opens up. The kitchen is big and functional; we sourced cabinetry from a friend renovating their own space, and topped it with leathered quartz — one of the best decisions of the project. Instead of running gas to the studio, we chose an induction cooktop for similar responsiveness.

In the back left corner, we created a bold bedroom design that is visually striking and intentionally flexible. Rather than walling it off, we used blinds so the space can change as needed. Built-in bookshelves on the interior side of the bedroom wall make use of the space and avoid furniture clutter.

The rich back corner houses a spacious bathroom with a floating sink, room for a hamper, and a stacked washer and dryer. The entire back half of the space was raised primarily to accommodate plumbing, and also to create a split-level feel that makes the layout more interesting.

We framed in the garage door opening on the interior and added a French door, giving the option for privacy or an open layout. We used open-cell insulation in the walls and closed-cell insulation between the rafters. After applying a fire-retardant barrier, we sprayed the ceiling paint and repeated the process until the color was just right.

We painted the walls a warm white dove and painted the floor with the thought of maybe returning later for tile or hardwood.

This project was incredibly fun and rewarding from concept to completion. It ended up fantastic. It's small but feels palatial for one — well designed without being overthought, and simply a peaceful place to just be.
